Tip for audio recordings: Ask everyone to state their name at the start of the meeting — "Hi, I'm Jane Smith, strata council president." MinuteHand will pre-fill speaker names automatically.

Independent review

Built for B.C. Strata Councils — and stress-tested to prove it

Under B.C. Standard Bylaw 19, councils must distribute minutes to all owners within two weeks of the meeting. MinuteHand produces a professional, legally structured draft in under 30 seconds.

Attendance & voting accuracy

Tracks when members arrive late and restricts their participation to votes held after they joined — preserving legally defensible voting records.

B.C. legal terminology

Distinguishes "Units" from official "Strata Lots" and flags where numbers should be verified against the strata roll before warning letters or fines are issued.

Financial complexity

Formats contractor quotes into clean comparison tables and correctly categorizes emergency expenditures (Section 98) versus items requiring a Special General Meeting.

Legal liability flags

When council members make unverified claims, MinuteHand inserts professional caveats recommending engineer or legal review before the minutes are finalized.

Frequently asked questions

How much does MinuteHand cost?

MinuteHand offers pay-per-use credits ($7 CAD per meeting, or $20 for 5 credits — about $4 per meeting) and the Board Plan ($75 CAD per year). Credits never expire. The Board Plan includes 12 meeting credits plus the full Governance Records suite.

What's included in the Board Plan?

The Board Plan ($75 CAD/year) includes 12 meeting credits per year plus the full Governance Records suite: a motion and decision registry, cross-meeting action item tracking, auto-generated next-meeting agendas, full-text search across all meeting content, a council roster with role history, a special resolution vote calculator, a governance document library with version history, governance document templates (AGM Notice, SGM Notice, Written Resolution), and an owner-facing minutes portal with a secure per-organization link.

A 30-day full refund is available — no questions asked.

Can I upgrade from pay-per-use to the Board Plan later?

Yes. You can upgrade to the Board Plan at any time from within your account. Your existing credit balance carries over — the Board Plan's 12 included credits add on top of what you already have. All previously generated minutes remain accessible.

Does the Board Plan replace pay-per-use credits?

No. The Board Plan includes 12 meeting credits per year — enough for a typical strata council that meets monthly. If you generate more meetings than that, you can buy additional credits at the same pay-per-use rates. Credits never expire.

What is the refund policy?

Board Plan purchases include a 30-day full refund window — no questions asked. Pay-per-use credits are non-refundable once used. Contact hello@minutehand.ca.

What file formats does MinuteHand support?

MinuteHand accepts pasted or typed notes, Microsoft Word (.docx) files, and audio recordings in .mp3, .m4a, or .wav format up to 50 MB. Audio recordings are automatically transcribed with speaker identification.

Is my meeting data private?

Yes. Your notes and audio are used only to generate your minutes and are not stored beyond what is needed for processing. Audio files are deleted after transcription. Your data is never sold or used to train AI models.

Who is MinuteHand for?

MinuteHand is designed for volunteer secretaries and board members of strata and condo councils, homeowners associations (HOAs), and nonprofit organizations — anyone responsible for meeting minutes without administrative support.

How accurate is the transcription?

MinuteHand uses Deepgram's Nova-2 speech recognition model, which is highly accurate for meeting recordings. For best results, ask participants to state their name at the start of the meeting. You can review and confirm speaker names before generating minutes.

Do MinuteHand credits expire?

No. MinuteHand credits never expire. Buy a pack and use them whenever you need them — once a week, once a quarter, or whenever your council meets.
